Compare sites and landing dates, side by side.

Up to four scenarios: a site, a landing date, the height of the panels. The better value of each column is marked.

  • Power: share of the mission in sunlight, longest night
  • Link: windows of direct contact with the Earth, longest gap
  • The sky the lander sees, on the real skyline, moving in time

Where? A map computed live in the browser.

At this moment: Sun, Earth or both in view. Shadows move as time plays.

Over the mission: the share of time with power and link together.

Lunar Landing Site Navigator4 / 7

When? Every landing day of the coming year.

A schedule slips, and the question changes from "where" to "when". Each column is a landing day: the brighter, the longer power, the link, or both last unbroken after touchdown.

  • The best landing days are marked
  • Eclipses of the Sun by the Earth are included
  • A link reproduces any comparison exactly

Built on NASA data, checked against NASA.

Every skyline comes from the LOLA elevation model of NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter, at 120 m. The results are tested against NASA's own.

0.004°

largest difference from NASA/JPL Horizons in the direction of the Sun and the Earth, over 9,810 reference values

0.98

correlation with the NASA LOLA illumination maps at 400 places, for sunlight and for Earth visibility

8.3 s

largest difference from NASA's eclipse predictions: 58 contact times of the 14 lunar eclipses of 2025–2030

3

real landings as checks: IM-1, Chandrayaan-3, and the eclipse seen by Blue Ghost Mission 1

317 automated tests. Every figure can be reproduced with a command given in the repository. Geometry only: a planning aid, not a flight product.
