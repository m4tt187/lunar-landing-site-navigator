When? Every landing day of the coming year.

A schedule slips, and the question changes from "where" to "when". Each column is a landing day: the brighter, the longer power, the link, or both last unbroken after touchdown.

  • The best landing days are marked
  • Eclipses of the Sun by the Earth are included
  • A link reproduces any comparison exactly

Which? Describe the mission, get the answer.

So many days of operations, a lander that survives so many hours of darkness: every site with a real skyline and every landing hour of a year are searched, and the best site-and-date pairs are ranked.

  • Ranked by power for the whole mission first
  • Then by contact with the Earth
  • One click puts an answer into the comparison

largest difference from NASA/JPL Horizons in the direction of the Sun and the Earth, over 9,810 reference values

0.98

correlation with the NASA LOLA illumination maps at 400 places, for sunlight and for Earth visibility

largest difference from NASA's eclipse predictions over 58 contact times of 13 eclipses of 2025–2030 (52 s for the one that grazes the Moon)

real landings as checks: IM-1, Chandrayaan-3, and the eclipse seen by Blue Ghost Mission 1

South of 80° S every skyline comes from NASA's LOLA elevation model, at 120 m around each site. 317 automated tests; every figure reproducible from the repository. Geometry only: a planning aid, not a flight product.

  • Mission planners shortlist sites and dates in seconds, with numbers traceable to NASA sources
  • Educators show why the pole is hard: the Sun circling the horizon, a ridge hiding it for days
  • Anyone picks a place on the Moon and sees what a lander would see
